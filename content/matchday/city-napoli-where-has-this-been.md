For the last 10 minutes of this game, Manchester City played like Manchester City. Everyone looked hungry, the ball moved, and Cherki scored in the 86th minute to make it 2-2. It was the best we've played offensively in 5 games. And it only happened after Napoli had gone ahead twice. We had 63% of the ball, 18 shots to their 9, and 61 touches in their box to their 20, and it still took going behind to wake us up. Now that we'd been scored on, there was intent and urgency. So where the hell has this been for weeks?

The new back four is the good news. Nunes, Guéhi, Gvardiol and O'Reilly, with Gvardiol finally back at center back, and he looked so much better there. I'm so much happier with him there than at full back. Nunes is a revelation at right back compared to Khusanov. That's not a knock on Khusanov, he's just not a right back. Nunes had 110 touches, the most on our team, and completed 90 of 95 passes, and his work with Cherki on the right is turning into a real partnership. In the 47th minute Napoli were through on goal and he defended it brilliantly. We still got caught in transition, but that's the name of the game with Maresca ball, so I'm not mad about it. Everybody was hungry to defend, and it looked a lot more organized.

Elliot Anderson higher up at the 8 is a revelation, again. That's the KDB role, and with Kevin De Bruyne himself coming home in a Napoli shirt, it was fitting. By the 35th minute Anderson had put 3 balls in from the right-side channel from extremely dangerous positions. He finished with 4 shots, 3 on target, and 0.61 xG, the most on our team, and he forced a great save late on. He missed one of our 2 big chances too. I'll take that trade every game if it means he's up the pitch instead of anchoring at the 6. It was great to see Kevin De Bruyne, by the way. The Etihad gave him a huge reception, and he's still at his best whenever he's involved in play. You can tell we miss someone like that, but Anderson is stepping up.

<div class="md-player md-l" data-n="AM"><header><div><small>Enzo · Attacking mid</small><b>Fernández</b></div><i>48<em>My rating</em></i></header><dl><div><dt>Chances created</dt><dd>2</dd></div><div><dt>Ground duels</dt><dd>2/5</dd></div><div><dt>Subbed off</dt><dd>63'</dd></div></dl><p>We're still compensating for him.</p></div>

Before kickoff I said I loved Enzo as the lone 10. By halftime I'd changed my mind. Cherki was getting lost out on the right, which is definitely not his position, and you could see him drifting into the middle over and over. We're still compensating for Enzo instead of putting Cherki at the 10. Enzo put in a bad ball from a Cherki-won free kick in the 21st minute and didn't do much else before Ndiaye replaced him in the 63rd. That sub was great, because it moved Cherki into the middle and put Ndiaye on the right. On the other side, Allan was up and down the pitch all night. He's a hard worker and he made the most of the little he got. I didn't want him off, and at 55 minutes Doku replaced him. He played well. No need to have his head down. This was squad rotation, though. Doku is obviously the starting left winger, and he probably starts Sunday at Arsenal. But Allan and Ndiaye are the kind of wingers I want in the squad for games against real opposition, because they work back.

Then the same old story. We're playing out of the back, Cherki gives it away trying to combine with Guéhi, and Rasmus Højlund scores in the 55th minute off a Billy Gilmour assist. It was a great goal, to be fair, but it came out of nowhere. Could Donnarumma have done a little better on it? Possibly. FotMob and Sofascore both have him at -1.32 goals prevented, and I think they're wrong. He made a great save in the 43rd, and relative to the last few games he looked so much better. Napoli finished with 0.71 xG and 3 shots on target, and 2 of those 3 went in. With a lot of game left I wasn't feeling confident, because we just couldn't create anything substantial. That's been the frustration for weeks.

Then BOUADDI in the 67th minute, off a corner, with Doku doing the work to set it up. That's the first well-worked corner all season. Bouaddi struggled in the first half and needed to settle down and compose himself, which is valid for a 19-year-old. He did, and FotMob made him an 8.3. Doku was lively from the moment he came on. He completed 8 of 11 dribbles and created 4 chances in 35 minutes, and FotMob made him player of the match off the bench. He was cooking his defender every time he got it.

And then, right as we were rolling, Napoli went ahead again. Noa Lang hit a great volley in the 76th minute, but Nico O'Reilly is fully, 1000% at fault for it. That was terrible, absolutely horrific. He had a much better first half than at the weekend, and FotMob still gave him a 7.0, but the left back position needs addressing. As good as Gvardiol and O'Reilly are going forward, Nico has been a liability defensively this season, and Gvardiol is a much more poised center back. Who fixes it? That's a Maresca question. O'Reilly can play there, and he can defend. He's just playing boneheaded right now.

<div class="md-tiles md-r"><p>The last 10 minutes<b>Knocking on the door</b></p><div><strong>1</strong><span>Gvardiol shot off the post</span></div><div><strong>86'</strong><span>Cherki equalizer, Ndiaye assist</span></div><div><strong>14</strong><span>Haaland touches all game</span></div></div>

We could not afford to lose this game, and for the first time in weeks we played like it. Gvardiol hit the post, Anderson forced a great save, and in the 86th minute Cherki finished it off from an Ndiaye assist. Cherki finished with 12 touches in their box and a goal, and FotMob had him at 7.9. The one guy who still isn't in the game is Haaland: 14 touches and 1 shot all night. He's not in the form he needs to be in, and he's feeding off scraps. I'm starting to get worried. He looks visibly frustrated, like he's not satisfied with the direction of the club, and he needs to get back into goal-scoring form soon. A draw at home to Napoli isn't a disaster. What bugs me is that we know what this team looks like when it actually wants it, and it took going 2-1 down to see it.
