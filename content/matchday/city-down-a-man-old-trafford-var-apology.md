City won the derby 1-0 at Old Trafford playing with ten men for the final 67 minutes, and the goal that decided it was bad enough that the Premier League's own officiating body apologized to United afterward. Sit with both of those at once. That's not a clean win. It's the kind of result that tells you more about the team than a comfortable one would.

Start with the red card, because it's the whole shape of the game. Foden went down, Bruno Fernandes kicked him on the ground, kicked him again, and Foden kicked back at his midsection getting up. One card got shown. Maresca said it after the game better than I could: "It can be a red card but in the same action can be a red card for Bruno." He's right, and I don't think it's close. My actual verdict, for what it's worth: both of them should have gotten a yellow, not a red. It's a derby, tempers run hot on both sides by design, and I think games like this earn the referee a little more leeway before a card decides the whole outcome, not less. Doesn't matter now. Twenty-three minutes in, City were down a man at Old Trafford in the derby, and the actual game started from there.

Then the goal. Haaland finished on sixty minutes, it stood, City won 1-0, and a few days later the refs' body admitted it shouldn't have. Enzo was in an offside position in the buildup, the on-field call was that he didn't play the ball, and the review body later said VAR never properly weighed whether his position affected United's goalkeeper. That's not a fan complaining. That's the people who run the officiating owning that they got it wrong. I'm not going to pretend that doesn't matter. It does. City took the points and a real apology in the same week, and both of those things are true. It doesn't sour the win for me, though, honestly. I don't have much interest in what PGMOL thinks after the fact, and City have been on the wrong end of a call like this against this exact opponent before, by my memory. It goes both ways over enough years. I'll take a derby win at Old Trafford with ten men in any era, under any circumstance.

I'm not going to pretend the ten men in white did nothing to deserve staying in the game, though. United had fifty-five percent of the ball, sixteen shots to six, and by the raw shot count it should have been more one-sided than it was. But City had the moment that mattered, and they had Donnarumma, who saved this result as much as anyone. A point-blank stop on Mbeumo in stoppage time, and before that a full-stretch save on a Mainoo curler from twenty-five yards that beat him and hit the post instead of going in. Take either of those away and this is a draw at best. That's not a defense of the finished product, that's just what happened on the pitch, and it happened because City had to defend a one-goal lead a man short against a team with the ball for over half the game.

I said Cherki for Man of the Match before kickoff, and that read never really got a fair test. City went down to ten men in the twenty-third minute and the entire shape of the game changed before he had a real chance to take it over. He got taken off at halftime for Ndiaye, who came on with real hunger. Nunes, though, was an absolute beast from start to finish, defending Rashford one-on-one for most of the second half and winning that matchup on effort as much as anything, and Anderson deserves to be standing right next to him. First derby, started ahead of Bouaddi, and played like two players out there. Bouaddi's a serious talent, but he isn't at the point yet where you hand him a game that turns into what this one turned into, down a man on the road in the derby. Anderson had the experience for that specific kind of afternoon, and it showed. Guehi had his moment too, and I mean that literally, seventy-one minutes in he bodied Rashford off the ball in a way that should end up on a highlight reel of its own. Foden's red obviously cut his day short, but before that he was doing exactly what I thought he'd do to Luke Shaw, and it's a shame we didn't get to see where that went for another sixty-plus minutes.

I had Enzo down as a bad one watching it live, and I want to walk that back some. Too high up the pitch and disconnected for the first twenty minutes or so, that part's real. But once City went down to ten men and the game turned into exactly the kind of match where two central midfielders have to do the work of three, Enzo was good. Him and Anderson outfought United's three across the middle of the pitch for most of the afternoon, a two-man engine room beating a three-man one. He was off in the eighty-second minute for O'Reilly, but by then the job was mostly done. My patience with him is genuinely high right now. He's not a typical City player, the profile's different from what this team usually runs through midfield, and I'm still working out exactly what he is on the ball. That's a good problem to have, not a pattern I'm worried about yet. Haaland did what Haaland does, one real chance, buries it, doesn't need a second. And whatever you think about the goal that decided it, City are now unbeaten in six Premier League games with a man sent off going back to 2021, five wins and a draw. That's a real number, not a vibe, and it says something about how this group handles adversity that shows up over and over, not just on Sunday.

I called this one wrong on the scoreline. I had City winning comfortably, two goals plus, maybe four. What actually happened was tighter, uglier, and honestly more interesting than the version I predicted, because a comfortable City win doesn't tell you anything about the team you didn't already know. A 1-0 grind at Old Trafford with ten men for two thirds of the match tells you they can be genuinely outplayed statistically and still find a way to be standing at the final whistle. It also tells you City are getting help they probably shouldn't be getting, and I'd rather say that plainly than pretend the only story here is heart and resilience. Both things happened. The result counts either way, and City are three points better off in a title race where three points is going to matter in May a lot more than how they were earned in September.
