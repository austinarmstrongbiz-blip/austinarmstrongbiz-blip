City won the derby 1-0 at Old Trafford playing with ten men for the final 67 minutes, and the goal that decided it was bad enough that the Premier League's own officiating body apologized to United afterward. Sit with both of those at once. That's not a clean win. It's the kind of result that tells you more about the team than a comfortable one would.

Start with the red card, because it's the whole shape of the game. Foden went down, Bruno Fernandes kicked him on the ground, kicked him again, and Foden kicked back at his midsection getting up. One card got shown. Maresca said it after the game better than I could: "It can be a red card but in the same action can be a red card for Bruno." He's right, and I don't think it's close. Doesn't matter now. Twenty-three minutes in, City were down a man at Old Trafford in the derby, and the actual game started from there.

Then the goal. Haaland finished on sixty minutes, it stood, City won 1-0, and a few days later the refs' body admitted it shouldn't have. Enzo was in an offside position in the buildup, the on-field call was that he didn't play the ball, and the review body later said VAR never properly weighed whether his position affected United's goalkeeper. That's not a fan complaining. That's the people who run the officiating owning that they got it wrong. I'm not going to pretend that doesn't matter. It does. City took the points and a real apology in the same week, and both of those things are true.

I'm not going to pretend the ten men in white did nothing to deserve staying in the game, though. United had fifty-five percent of the ball, sixteen shots to six, and by the raw shot count it should have been more one-sided than it was. But City had the moment that mattered, and they had Donnarumma, who saved this result as much as anyone. A point-blank stop on Mbeumo in stoppage time, and before that a full-stretch save on a Mainoo curler from twenty-five yards that beat him and hit the post instead of going in. Take either of those away and this is a draw at best. That's not a defense of the finished product, that's just what happened on the pitch, and it happened because City had to defend a one-goal lead a man short against a team with the ball for over half the game.

Nunes was the actual best player on the pitch, not the guy I called before kickoff. I said Cherki for Man of the Match, and Cherki got taken off at halftime. That's on me, not on him necessarily, sometimes a read is just wrong. But Nunes at right back, defending Rashford one-on-one for most of the second half, winning that matchup on effort as much as anything, that's the derby performance nobody's going to talk about outside the numbers. Guehi had his moment too, and I mean that literally, seventy-one minutes in he bodied Rashford off the ball in a way that should end up on a highlight reel of its own. Foden's red obviously cut his day short, but before that he was doing exactly what I thought he'd do to Luke Shaw, and it's a shame we didn't get to see where that went for another sixty-plus minutes.

Enzo had a bad one, and I'm not going to dress it up. Too high up the pitch early, not involved, looked like he was playing himself back into shape in real time rather than actually influencing the game, and he was off in the eighty-second minute for O'Reilly. For a hundred and thirty-five million pounds you're allowed one quiet derby. You're not allowed to look lost in it, and for stretches on Sunday he did. Quick hits on the rest: Ndiaye came on for Cherki at the half and played with the kind of hunger you want from a sub in a derby. Haaland did what Haaland does, one real chance, buries it, doesn't need a second. And whatever you think about the goal that decided it, City are now unbeaten in six Premier League games with a man sent off going back to 2021, five wins and a draw. That's a real number, not a vibe, and it says something about how this group handles adversity that shows up over and over, not just on Sunday.

I called this one wrong on the scoreline. I had City winning comfortably, two goals plus, maybe four. What actually happened was tighter, uglier, and honestly more interesting than the version I predicted, because a comfortable City win doesn't tell you anything about the team you didn't already know. A 1-0 grind at Old Trafford with ten men for two thirds of the match tells you they can be genuinely outplayed statistically and still find a way to be standing at the final whistle. It also tells you City are getting help they probably shouldn't be getting, and I'd rather say that plainly than pretend the only story here is heart and resilience. Both things happened. The result counts either way, and City are three points better off in a title race where three points is going to matter in May a lot more than how they were earned in September.
