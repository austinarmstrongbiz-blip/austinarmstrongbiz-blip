City beat Norwich 5-0 in the third round of the Carabao Cup, three players made their first City appearances in Rulli, Samba, and Allan, and the number I called before kickoff was 4-1, maybe 5. It landed at 5-0. Norwich never had a shot on target, City had 66% of the ball and 1.38 xG to their 0.12, and by any measure it was exactly the kind of night you'd expect against a Championship side's rotated lineup. That's not the story, though. The story is what the night did to two very different reputations at the same time.

Start with Floyd Samba, because he's the reason anyone's talking about this game at all. 17 years old, son of Chris Samba, first senior start, and he heads one in off Allan's cross in the 29th minute, then curls a second into the bottom corner in the 54th after Norwich gave the ball away in their own third. Two goals on debut is the kind of stat line that writes its own headline. What it doesn't show is that he was doing this against a Championship back line that had no answer for his pace or his decision-making in either box, and that's a real thing at 17, not a small thing.

> It was like a man playing with boys in terms of how quick and the decision-making he was making.

That's Rayan Cherki, not Samba, and that's the part of the night that got buried under the scoreline. Cherki set up the third goal with a run that turned a Bouaddi through-ball into a finish from the edge of the box, then got subbed off before half was even out, and Fanalysis had him rated the lowest of anyone who started outfield at 65. That number is about minutes, not quality. The entire game flowed through him while he was on the pitch, and if you rate it per 90 instead of per appearance, that's a 74, not a 65. He looked like the best player on the field for the time he had it, and the rating just doesn't have a way to say that.

Rayan Ait-Nouri had the other half of the good news. He set up Allan's goal in the 48th minute with a pass that took out two defenders, and afterward Austin doubled down on the read he had going in: this isn't a guy playing out of position at left back, this is a guy playing an inverted role that lets him pinch inside like Rico Lewis does on the opposite side, and it works because the width on that flank is coming from someone else stepping outside instead. Against Norwich's Championship lineup, sure, that's an easier proving ground than the Premier League. But the shape held up, the end product was real, and that's not nothing at 24 years old trying to force his way into a City XI that already has options out wide.

The rest of the bench was a mixed bag: McAidoo finished well off O'Reilly's work for the fifth in stoppage time, and Kaden Braithwaite got caught out of position twice in a few minutes as a late sub at center back, one of them a bad step that only didn't cost a goal because Norwich were offside. Fanalysis gave him a 42, the lowest number on the pitch. He had a good preseason and real expectations on him, but most of his reps have come at outside back, not center back, and the positioning on both of those plays was bad enough that it's worth naming plainly. He's got real talent. He also has work to do.

Allan's night is the closer, and it's the most encouraging arc of the four debutants because of how it moved. First half on the right, he wasn't sharp, didn't look totally comfortable. Then McAidoo came on for the right side and Allan shifted left, and from there he looked like a different player, more willing to take people on, more direct, and he got his goal off the switch. First appearance in English football, first game in a City shirt, and the expectation going in was tempered because of that. He grew into it in real time on the same night, which is a better signal than a player who's good from minute one and never has to adjust anything.

And then Rico Lewis, who's the one name in this XI that didn't get a good night out of a 5-0 win. A 62 rating, the lowest of anyone who started an outfield position for the full 90, and the honest read on him right now isn't complicated: he should have left this past summer. He wanted to stay and fight for his spot, and that's a fair thing for a player to want, but the technical level isn't where it needs to be for him to actually progress forward in this City team. He gets into the right spaces, that part is real, that's why Pep liked him and why Maresca still plays him at right back. The problem is everything on the other side of that: the link-up play, the final product, the decision-making in the moments that actually matter, and he's not physical enough to make up the difference with anything else. If he wants a real future at this club instead of just a place on the fringes of it, that's the part that has to change, not the part where he finds space.

This was a B-team beating a B-team, and the gap in the middle of the pitch is why it never felt close. Kovačić and Bouaddi controlled the game from deep, and Vitor Reis and Khusanov formed a center-back pairing that looked settled for two players that young playing together for the first time. Put that alongside Cherki pulling the strings and Samba finishing everything in front of him, and the spine running Reis-Khusanov-Kovačić-Bouaddi-Cherki-Samba through the middle of the pitch is exactly what you want backup and fringe players to look like when they're pushing for a spot in the first team. Norwich could compete in spurts. The quality through the middle is what decided it.

Norwich's Championship lineup was never going to make this a hard night for City, and the scoreline says exactly that. But a 5-0 tells you almost nothing on its own, and the four players who actually mattered here, Samba's finishing, Cherki's control of the game while he had it, Ait-Nouri's case for a bigger role, and Lewis's honest ceiling problem, told you plenty. That's the version of this result worth remembering, not the number next to it.
